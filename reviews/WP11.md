So the aha is real and strong.

But the cell has two cases where its own output contradicts the Why ("blanking the line right before it moves it a lot"):

1. **Short closing lines are used as-is.** The cell uses the participant's meeting unchanged whenever its last line has 4 words or fewer ("already ends with a short, context-dependent reply"). A meeting that ends "Okay, thanks everyone." (3 words) gave **0.0%** for line 1 and **0.2%** for the line before last, so the cell prints `Changing line 1: last answer moved 0%` and `Changing the line before last: last answer moved 0%` right above a sentence saying the second one "moves it a lot". "Thanks, bye." and "Great, that's all." are normal endings.
2. **A meeting that already ends with the same pattern weakens it.** My own spoken meeting ends "Ravi, can you order them by Friday? / Sure, I will do it tonight."; the cell appends the fixed exchange after it and printed `Changing line 1: last answer moved 0%`, `Changing the line before last: last answer moved 13%` (run P), again contradicting "moves it a lot".

**Fix (a few lines):** always append a fresh exchange to a neutral prefix, for example the shipped sample meeting or the participant's first four lines (not their last two), so the test is the 0.0% against 94 to 95% I measured; or print the two numbers and make the Why conditional ("if the line before last moved it much more than line 1, that is the previous-turn effect; if both are small, the last line did not depend on it").
